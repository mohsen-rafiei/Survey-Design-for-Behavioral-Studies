## What this is

A working checklist for anyone building a questionnaire for a behavioral study: graduate students, lab members, UX and human factors researchers, and anyone who has to review a survey before it goes into the field.

The checklist has 78 items organized by the order in which an instrument is built. Each item names a mistake that recurs in the survey methodology literature, states what to do instead, and gives the source. The 23 items with the strongest and most frequently replicated evidence are marked with an asterisk and should be settled before anything else. Every claim traces to one of 113 published papers, each linked through its DOI.

## What is inside

| Part | Stage | Items | Example of a mistake covered |
|:---:|---|:---:|---|
| A | Before writing any items: purpose and measurement | 8 | Planning group comparisons on a scale without a measurement invariance test |
| B | Writing the questions | 12 | Double-barreled questions that ask two things and allow one answer |
| C | Choosing response formats | 10 | Agree-disagree statements as the default format |
| D | Assembling the questionnaire: length, order, layout and device | 11 | The same item order for every respondent |
| E | Pretesting | 8 | One round of cognitive interviews, then fielding |
| F | Sampling and recruiting | 10 | Reporting prevalence from a convenience sample as a population estimate |
| G | Preventing and detecting low-quality responses | 11 | Dropping everyone who fails a single attention check |
| H | Analysis decisions that start at the design stage | 8 | Parametric tests on single Likert items |

After the eight parts, the checklist collects the figures worth remembering in one table, lists seven papers to read first and in what order, and gives the full reference list.

## How to use it

1. Open [survey-design-checklist.md](survey-design-checklist.md) and work through the parts in order while drafting the instrument. Tick each box when the item has been dealt with or judged not to apply.
2. To get clickable boxes, copy a part into a GitHub issue in your own project, or paste the whole file into a notes app that renders Markdown task lists.
3. To adapt it for your own lab or course, fork the repository, edit the file, and keep the citation below.

## A few of the numbers

| Finding | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Undergraduates responding carelessly on a long survey taken for credit | 10% to 12% | Meade et al., 2012 |
| Online survey articles published in 2022 with no data-quality check at all | 55% | Gottfried, 2024 |
| Group comparisons that had been tested for measurement invariance | 4% | Maassen et al., 2023 |
| Usable number of scale points | 5 to 7, with no gain beyond 6 | Simms et al., 2019 |
| Accuracy of self-report immediately after a task, without and with memory aids | 44% without; 78% to 89% with | Manzke et al., 2025 |
| Responses excluded as fraudulent across online health studies | 3% to 94% | Comachio et al., 2024 |
